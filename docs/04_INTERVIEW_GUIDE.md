# 04 — Interview Guide

**Duration:** 30–40 minutes  
**Mode:** Semi-structured discovery  
**Do not pitch the product during the discovery portion.**

## Opening and consent

“Thank you for speaking with me. I’m conducting independent research into how commercial EV delivery fleets prepare vehicles for shifts and coordinate energy replenishment. I’m interested in understanding your current process and recent experiences, not selling a product. I’ll take notes. I will not publish your name, company-sensitive information, or identifiable operational details without your permission. Is it okay to proceed?”

Do not record audio/video without explicit permission.

## 1. Role and context

- What is your role in fleet operations?
- Which decisions do you personally make?
- What kinds of vehicles and replenishment models are involved?
- How many depots, shifts, or operating teams are relevant to your work?
- Which responsibilities sit with your organisation versus third parties?

## 2. Reconstruct a real shift

- Think about the most recent shift you prepared. What happened from start to dispatch?
- Who checked vehicle condition and energy availability?
- How was a vehicle marked ready?
- What information did you need, and where did it come from?
- Which steps were manual?
- What changed at the last minute?

## 3. Recent failure / exception

- Tell me about the most recent case where a planned vehicle was unavailable or delayed.
- When did the team first know?
- What was the root cause?
- How did the team respond?
- Who coordinated it?
- What happened to the shift or deliveries?
- How often has something similar happened recently?

Ask for a concrete example. Do not accept a hypothetical answer as evidence of frequency.

## 4. Existing systems and workarounds

- Which spreadsheets, messaging tools, fleet systems, telematics, or charger platforms are used?
- Which system is treated as the source of truth?
- Where do systems disagree?
- What information is copied manually?
- What do you do when data is missing or stale?
- What would break if one tool were unavailable?

## 5. Business impact and buying process

- How do you measure dispatch readiness today?
- What is the consequence of a missed dispatch?
- How is the cost calculated, if at all?
- Who owns this metric?
- Who would evaluate and approve an additional software tool?
- What procurement, security, or integration requirements apply?
- What would make a new tool not worth adopting?

## 6. Close

- What have I misunderstood about the process?
- Who else sees this workflow from a different perspective?
- Would you be comfortable reviewing a workflow prototype later?

## Interviewer discipline

- Ask “what happened?” before “what would you like?”
- Ask “when was the last time?” before “how often?”
- Separate direct observation from participant interpretation.
- Do not suggest a feature and then count agreement as demand.
- Record disconfirming evidence and inconvenient findings.
- Do not promise a product or pilot.
