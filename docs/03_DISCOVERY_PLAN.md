# 03 — Customer Discovery Plan

## Objective

Determine whether a specific fleet segment has a frequent, consequential dispatch-readiness problem that is poorly handled by current processes and worth solving with software.

## Research design

Exploratory qualitative discovery. An initial target of 10–12 conversations is a recruitment goal, not a statistically representative sample and not a completed research result.

### Participant mix to seek

| Participant | Initial recruitment target | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Fleet owner / operations head | 3–4 | Business impact, budget, buying process |
| Depot manager / dispatcher | 3–4 | Actual shift-preparation workflow |
| Driver / rider | 2–3 | Frontline communication and reporting burden |
| Charging / swapping / technology operator | 1–2 | Energy availability and data dependencies |

Participants should have direct, recent experience with commercial EV fleet operations. Avoid relying only on EV enthusiasts, students, or people who do not perform the workflow.

## Recruitment

Potential recruitment channels:
- direct professional outreach to fleet operations leaders;
- local logistics and last-mile delivery networks;
- EV fleet operators and depot managers;
- charging / battery-swap providers;
- referrals from participants, with no pressure to disclose confidential information.

An organisation named as a recruitment target is not a participant, endorsement, or customer.

## Method

- 30–40 minute semi-structured interview.
- Ask about the last real shift, latest incident, and current workaround.
- Request a workflow walkthrough or anonymised artifact only if the participant is comfortable.
- Ask permission before recording or retaining identifiable details.
- Store participant identifiers separately from names/contact information.
- Do not publish company-sensitive information or quotes without explicit permission.

## Research questions

1. Walk me through the last shift you prepared.
2. How were vehicles selected and assigned?
3. How did you know each vehicle was ready?
4. Tell me about the most recent vehicle that was not ready at dispatch.
5. How was the issue discovered?
6. What caused it?
7. Who acted, and how long did resolution take?
8. What was the effect on dispatch, delivery, cost, or utilisation?
9. Which tools or people held the relevant information?
10. What was manually reconciled?
11. What information was stale, missing, or disputed?
12. Which current system would you keep if you could only keep one?
13. Who decides whether to purchase new operations software?
14. What integration, security, or operational constraints matter?
15. What would make a new tool too burdensome to adopt?

Avoid leading questions such as “Would a smart dashboard help?” until the existing workflow and problem are understood.

## Hypotheses

- H1 — Readiness visibility: the team cannot quickly establish which scheduled vehicles meet the requirements for the next shift.
- H2 — Fragmentation: relevant information is split across tools and people.
- H3 — Exception timing: blockers are discovered too late for effective intervention.
- H4 — Consequence: readiness failures create measurable operational impact.
- H5 — Adoption: a new coordination workflow can fit existing roles and systems at acceptable cost.

## Proposed decision thresholds

These are project decision rules, not industry benchmarks:
- At least 6 of 10 relevant operator-side participants independently describe a recent related problem.
- At least 4 participants can walk through a concrete workflow or recent incident.
- Multiple participants can describe a consequence beyond general inconvenience.
- At least 3 qualified participants agree to review a prototype or discuss a pilot.
- Any prototype usability threshold is pre-registered before testing.

Do not treat thresholds as proof by themselves. Sample composition, recency, contradictory evidence, and severity matter.

## Synthesis

For each theme, record:
- participant IDs and roles;
- verbatim quote only with permission;
- observed workflow;
- incident recency;
- reported frequency and consequence;
- workaround;
- evidence strength;
- counterexample;
- product implication;
- confidence and open questions.

## Exit criteria

Do not finalise the product wedge until you can state:
1. the specific user and operating context;
2. the current workflow and failure point;
3. why the failure matters;
4. why existing alternatives are insufficient;
5. what smallest intervention could help;
6. what evidence would disprove the thesis.
