# 01 — Market and Context

## Research question

What market and operating conditions make EV fleet readiness a relevant problem to investigate in Delhi-NCR, and what do those conditions actually prove?

## Working context

Commercial EV operations sit at the intersection of vehicle availability, energy replenishment, daily shift schedules, depot processes, and delivery commitments. A policy transition can increase the relevance of operational tooling, but policy alone does not prove demand for a particular software product.

## Policy context — use with care

The Delhi EV Policy 2026 and its implementation provisions should be read from the official Gazette / government publication. The policy's phased provisions have distinct vehicle categories and effective dates. Do not summarise them as a blanket immediate electrification mandate for every fleet or existing vehicle.

Before publishing a policy claim:
- link the official document, not a news summary alone;
- record publication / notification date and the relevant clause;
- quote the vehicle category and effective date precisely;
- distinguish new-registration requirements from fleet transition provisions;
- verify whether amendments, clarifications, or court orders changed the position.

**Current portfolio treatment:** policy is a possible market tailwind and research context, not proof of product-market fit.

## Market questions to answer

1. What types of last-mile operators run depot-based EV fleets in Delhi-NCR?
2. Which vehicle classes and replenishment models are used?
3. How do shift structures and depot operations differ by business model?
4. Who is accountable for readiness and dispatch exceptions?
5. Which software and hardware vendors already support these workflows?
6. What does the operator buy today, and what remains manual?
7. What operational cost can be attributed to readiness failures?
8. Who controls the budget and procurement decision?

## Market sizing approach

Do not publish a TAM/SAM/SOM number without a reproducible model. A credible bottom-up estimate should use:
- count of addressable operators in the defined geography and segment;
- qualifying fleet/depot count;
- proportion with the target workflow and ability to adopt;
- plausible annual contract value supported by buyer research;
- transparent source dates, exclusions, and sensitivity ranges.

Formula skeleton:

`SAM = Addressable operators \times Eligible share \times Plausible annual contract value`

This is a model structure, not a market estimate. The variables must be sourced or explicitly marked as assumptions.

## Primary-source research checklist

- Official Delhi EV Policy 2026 notification and subsequent amendments.
- Public fleet operator disclosures and case studies.
- Company product pages and product documentation.
- OCPP specifications from the Open Charge Alliance.
- Relevant procurement / tender documents where publicly available.
- Interviews with operators, dispatchers, drivers, and technology partners.
